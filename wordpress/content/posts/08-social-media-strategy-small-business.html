A social media marketing strategy is a plan for which platforms you’ll use, what you’ll post, how often, and how that activity turns into customers. Most small businesses don’t fail on social because of a lack of ideas — they fail because there’s no plan, so posting becomes random and stops. Here’s a realistic 90-day plan you can follow.

Days 1–14: foundations

Set one clear goal

Pick a goal you can measure: website enquiries, bookings, online sales or email sign-ups. Followers are a means, not the goal.

Choose your platforms

  • Instagram: visual products, lifestyle, local services, Reels.
  • Facebook: local communities, events, groups and older audiences.
  • LinkedIn: B2B services, professional expertise and hiring.
  • TikTok: short-form video and younger audiences.

Start with one or two. You can expand once the first is working.

Optimise your profiles

Consistent name and branding, a clear bio that says what you do and who for, a link to a relevant landing page, and contact details.

Days 15–30: content pillars and calendar

Content pillars are the 3–4 themes you’ll post about repeatedly. For example, a café might use behind the scenes, menu highlights, customer stories and local community. A B2B agency might use tips, case studies, team and industry news.

Days 31–60: publish, engage, learn

  • Post consistently on your calendar — batch-create content weekly.
  • Reply to comments and messages within a day; engagement is part of the algorithm and of customer service.
  • Engage with local businesses, partners and customers’ posts.
  • Track which posts drive saves, shares, profile visits and link clicks — not just likes.

Days 61–90: amplify and convert

  • Turn your best-performing organic posts into ads.
  • Retarget website visitors and video viewers with a clear offer.
  • Run one campaign per month tied to a specific product, service or promotion.
  • Review results and double down on the pillars and formats that convert.

What to measure

Reach and engagement tell you whether content resonates; website clicks, enquiries and sales tell you whether it pays. Review monthly and adjust the plan rather than abandoning it.

Conclusion

Social media works for small businesses when it’s focused, consistent and connected to an offer. Choose your platforms, commit to your pillars and calendar for 90 days, and let the data show you where to invest next.
