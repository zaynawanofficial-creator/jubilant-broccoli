Link building is the process of getting other websites to link to yours. Search engines treat each relevant link as a vote of confidence: the more trusted sites that reference your content, the more authoritative your site appears. In 2026, backlinks remain one of the strongest ranking factors — but how you get them matters more than ever.

Why backlinks matter for SEO

Google’s original breakthrough was using links to judge which pages deserve to rank. That principle still holds. Links help search engines discover your pages, understand what they’re about from the surrounding context and anchor text, and decide how much to trust them. In competitive niches, content alone rarely wins without authority behind it.

White-hat link building methods that work

Guest posting on relevant sites

Writing genuinely useful articles for respected blogs and publications in your niche, with a natural link back to a relevant page on your site.

Broken link building

Finding broken links on relevant sites and suggesting your content as a replacement. It helps the site owner and earns you a contextual link.

Digital PR and linkable assets

Original data, tools, guides and expert commentary that journalists and bloggers want to cite.

Business citations and profiles

Accurate listings on trusted directories and industry platforms. They’re foundational rather than powerful, but they build a natural profile and support local SEO.

Competitor link analysis

Studying which sites link to your competitors reveals proven opportunities for your own outreach.

Link building tactics to avoid

  • Buying hundreds of links from “packages” that never name the sites.
  • Private blog networks (PBNs) and link farms.
  • Automated comment, forum or profile spam.
  • Large-scale link exchanges and exact-match anchor text everywhere.

Google’s spam policies specifically call out link schemes. They’re not worth the risk.
