What actually drives the cost of SEO?

1. How competitive your market is

Ranking a plumber in one town is a different job from ranking a national insurance comparison site. The more established competitors you face, the more content and authority (backlinks) you need to overtake them — and that is where most of the budget goes.

2. The number of keywords and pages

Every keyword you target needs a page that genuinely deserves to rank: researched, written, optimised and internally linked. Five keywords and five pages is a very different workload from fifty.

3. Technical debt

Slow pages, broken links, duplicate content, indexing problems and poor mobile experience all hold rankings back. Sites with years of accumulated issues need more work up front. Google’s own Core Web Vitals guidance is a good benchmark for what “fast enough” means.

4. Content and link building

Content and backlinks are the two biggest variable costs. A package that includes written articles and real, manually outreached links will cost more than one that only tweaks meta tags — and it will also be the one that moves rankings.

5. Local vs national vs international

Local SEO focuses on your Google Business Profile, reviews, citations and location pages. National and international SEO needs more content, more authority and often multiple languages or regions.

Red flags: when cheap SEO gets expensive

  • Guaranteed #1 rankings. Nobody controls Google’s algorithm. Guarantees usually mean tricks that don’t last.
  • “1,000 backlinks for $50”. Bulk automated links break Google’s spam policies and can trigger penalties that take months to recover from.
  • No list of deliverables. If you can’t see exactly what will be done each month, you can’t hold anyone accountable.
  • Reports full of vanity metrics. Impressions and “visibility scores” mean little. You want rankings for keywords that convert, organic traffic and enquiries or sales.
  • Long lock-in contracts with no exit. Good agencies keep clients through results, not contracts.

How to judge whether SEO is worth it

Do the simple maths. Estimate how many extra visitors the target keywords could bring, multiply by your conversion rate and average customer value, and compare it with the monthly fee. A $499 package only needs to win a couple of clients a month for many service businesses to be profitable — and unlike paid ads, the traffic doesn’t stop when you stop paying.

Then give it time. Most sites see first movements within 4–8 weeks of technical and on-page fixes, with stronger gains at 3–6 months. If you need leads tomorrow, pair SEO with PPC while organic rankings build.
