What drives the cost of a website?

Number of pages and templates

Each unique page type — home, service, location, blog, product — needs designing and building. More templates mean more time.

Custom design vs template

A well-chosen template is fast and affordable. A fully custom design costs more but gives you a distinctive brand and conversion-focused layouts.

E-commerce features

Product catalogues, variations, filters, payment gateways, shipping rules and tax settings all add build and testing time.

Integrations

Booking systems, CRMs, email marketing, live chat and accounting tools need connecting and testing.

Content and SEO

Professional copywriting, photography and SEO setup add cost but make the difference between a site that looks good and one that ranks and converts. Our content writing service can supply page copy if you don’t have it.

Ongoing costs to budget for

  • Domain name: roughly $10–$20 per year.
  • Hosting: $5–$30 per month for most small business sites.
  • Premium plugins or apps: varies; many sites need none.
  • Maintenance: updates, backups and security — DIY or a care plan.

What every website should include (regardless of price)

  • Mobile-first, responsive design
  • Fast loading and good Core Web Vitals
  • SSL (HTTPS) and basic security hardening
  • Clear calls to action and contact options on every page
  • SEO foundations: titles, meta descriptions, headings, clean URLs and schema
  • Analytics and Google Search Console setup
  • An easy way for you to edit content

How to avoid overpaying (or underpaying)

Ask for a fixed, itemised quote; confirm who owns the site and content; check that hosting and plugins are in your name; and make sure SEO and speed aren’t treated as optional extras. A cheap site that doesn’t generate enquiries is the most expensive option of all.

Conclusion

Start with what the website needs to achieve — enquiries, bookings or online sales — and choose the simplest build that can do that well. You can always add pages and features as you grow.
