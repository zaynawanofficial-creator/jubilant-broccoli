Local SEO is how your business shows up when people nearby search for what you sell — the map pack, “near me” searches and Google Maps. Google says local results are ranked on three things: relevance, distance and prominence. You can’t move your shop, but you can absolutely improve relevance and prominence. This checklist is the exact order we work through for clients.

Google Business Profile (steps 1–7)

  1. Claim and verify your profile using the exact business name you trade under — no keyword stuffing.
  2. Choose the most accurate primary category. This is one of the strongest relevance signals. Add secondary categories only if they genuinely apply.
  3. Complete every field: hours, phone, website, services, products, attributes and a keyword-natural description.
  4. Add real photos of your premises, team and work. Update them regularly.
  5. Set your service areas accurately if you travel to customers.
  6. Post updates weekly or fortnightly — offers, projects, news. Activity signals a live business.
  7. Use the Q&A section to answer the questions customers actually ask before buying.

Reviews and reputation (steps 8–11)

  1. Ask every happy customer for a review with a direct link. Timing matters: ask right after a successful job.
  2. Reply to every review, good and bad, professionally and quickly.
  3. Never buy or gate reviews. Fake reviews break Google’s rules and, in the UK, consumer law.
  4. Build reviews on other trusted platforms relevant to your industry (Trustpilot, Yelp, industry directories).

Citations and NAP consistency (steps 12–14)

  1. Standardise your name, address and phone (NAP) and use the exact same format everywhere.
  2. List your business on major directories and data aggregators for your country.
  3. Fix duplicates and outdated listings — old addresses and phone numbers confuse Google and customers.

On-site local signals (steps 15–18)

  1. Create a dedicated page for each service and location you genuinely serve, with unique content — not copy-pasted city names.
  2. Add LocalBusiness schema with your NAP, hours and geo-coordinates.
  3. Embed your address and a map on your contact page and show NAP in the footer.
  4. Make it fast and mobile-friendly. Most local searches happen on phones.

Local authority (steps 19–20)

  1. Earn local links: sponsorships, local news, suppliers, chambers of commerce and community partnerships.
  2. Publish locally useful content — guides, case studies and FAQs that local customers search for.

How to track local SEO progress

Track map pack rankings for your main keywords from several points in your area, plus Google Business Profile calls, direction requests and website clicks. Those are the numbers that turn into revenue.
